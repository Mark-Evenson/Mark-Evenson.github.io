01 Case Study
PERSONAL PROJECT
SOLIDWORKS + FEA

Reverse-Engineered Suspension Redesign

Mazda CX-3 front lower control arm, reverse-engineered, then redesigned for rally-spec. FOS improved from 0.6 to 2.3 at equal mass

I hand-measured and photographed the front lower control arm off my dad's 2021 Mazda CX-3, then rebuilt it in SolidWorks. Baseline FEA under a worst-case cornering load showed the OEM part exceeding yield strength in several areas, so I redesigned it for rally use with a stronger alloy, added cross-bracing, and lightening holes to offset the added mass, then validated the result against the original with FEA.

Project origin

The project started when my dad and I were repairing his crashed CX-3 this summer, which gave me hands-on access to a real suspension component.

Measure the OEM part

Hand-measured the control arm in place on the car — overall length, subframe bolt spacing, bushing bore diameter, wall thickness, and general cross-section shape.

Model the as-is geometry

Rebuilt the part in SolidWorks from measurements, matching real mounting hardpoints so the model reflects actual chassis constraints.

Compare baseline & rally load cases

Established the OEM stress pattern under estimated street-driving cornering and braking, then increased the loading to a worst-case rally scenario combining cornering and static loads. Under that rally case, the 4.70 lb OEM arm reached 344 MPa maximum stress and a minimum FOS of ~0.6, confirming that it exceeded yield and needed reinforcement.

Upgrade material & add cross-bracing

Swapped from generic plain carbon steel (Sy ≈ 220 MPa) to AISI 4130 steel, normalized (Sy ≈ 460 MPa) — a real alloy used in motorsport fabrication — and added internal cross-bracing ribs spanning the open C-channel to resist twisting under cornering/braking loads, a common rally fabrication technique. Together these raised FOS from ~0.6 to ~1.9.

Add lightening holes

Removed material from confirmed low-stress zones with a row of lightening holes, bringing the redesign to 4.68 lb — 0.02 lb lighter than the 4.70 lb OEM baseline — while retaining the strength gained from the 4130 steel and cross-bracing.

Validate with FEA

Re-ran the final design under the same worst-case rally load case for an apples-to-apples comparison. Maximum stress fell from 344 MPa to 197 MPa, minimum FOS rose from ~0.6 to ~2.3, and mass changed from 4.70 lb to 4.68 lb.

Assumptions worth noting: baseline and rally loads were derived from estimated vehicle weight and a standard 0.8g cornering multiplier, not manufacturer data, and applied as idealized rigid loads (real rubber bushings would absorb some of this load in practice). With more time, I'd validate against real off-road telemetry, refine the rib geometry with topology optimization, and prototype the bushing interface for manufacturability.
